# Playtest guide 3 (2026-11-20)

The game: https://heirsofruin.github.io/WW1/ (refresh hard if the bottom bar has no Map tab).
This guide replaces PLAYTEST2-GUIDE.md. It says what is new since then, how to reach each new thing,
and what to send back.

## Sending a problem

Menu > **Save link (copy)**, paste the link with a screenshot and a line of what was wrong. The link
carries the whole game, so the exact screen can be replayed. (A long save code pasted into a phone
once came through cut short; the link and the in-page box do not have that fault.)

## What is new, and how to see it

| New | How to reach it | What to judge |
|---|---|---|
| **The soldier's words**: period words underlined; tap for a plain meaning | Any screen. Words in a choice are listed above the choices. All of them at the bottom of the Journal. Menu switches the underlines off | Too many underlines? A meaning that does not fit the sentence? A word you did not know that is not underlined? |
| **French and slang** | Any town; most at the estaminet at Ternecourt (1916 on) | Does it read naturally? Anything you could not follow even with a tap? |
| **The Map tab** | Bottom bar | Does it help? Is "You" where the story says you are? |
| **A man in front of you** | About half the advances and trench fights, and raids you go on | Do the four answers feel different? |
| **Rank that decides** | Lance corporal up: you pick who goes on a duty. Sergeant or officer: a fourth battle order. Soldier tab: "The next step" | Does a stripe now change what you do? |
| **Nerve costs on battle orders** | Every battle | Can you see a breakdown coming? |
| **The officer's servant** | Say yes on the second tour of 1915 (a private with no trade yet) | Is the trade clear? His officer's death? |
| **The tanks** | Say yes in January 1917. Offered to mill men, Lewis gunners, and about one other man in three (pit men now too) | The crew of *Polaris*; Cambrai; the Whippet (say yes to Pring in February 1918) |
| **The Flying Corps** | A Lewis gunner; refuse the tanks; say yes in May 1917 | The pilot and the fitter; being shot down |
| **The sweetheart and Marthe** | Letters from 1915; Marthe at Ternecourt from spring 1916 | The leaves, and every ending now tells her road |
| **More lasses** | From the second tour of 1915 (the baker's daughter behind Loos) | The ones you never see again; the shelled village |
| **Rivals** | Soldier tab > Rivals. Sergeant Maddock and Ted Hallam from 1915; Tea-time Otto and Stanley Rudge from 1916 | Do they matter by the end? |
| **Life behind the line** | Twelve new events in 1916 and 1917 | Enough story in those years now? |
| **Endings told in full** | Killed, wounded out, shell shock, taken | Still abrupt anywhere? |

## The balance now

Best play reaches the Armistice in about one game in ten (it was one in sixteen); three men in four
come through 1915. A man who always hangs back does worse than one who pulls his weight: the medical
officer believes a man the company thinks well of.

## Roads least tested by a person

A full tank game; a full air game; a servant game; any game that reaches 1918. The late events of the
rivals and the lasses have been read by forcing them, rarely in real play.
