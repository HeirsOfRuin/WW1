## History: what the game would use

Checked on 2026-11-14 by web search. As on 2026-11-12 (SOURCES.md), most sites could only be read
through search extracts, because the session's network blocked them. "Found" below means found in
those extracts. It does not mean the page was read in full.

**The corps.**
- **The name changed three times.** The Heavy Section, Machine Gun Corps, formed in 1916. It became
  the Heavy Branch in November 1916, then the Tank Corps in summer 1917. The Gazette transfers
  officers with effect from 28 July 1917; one source says 28 June. Use "the summer of 1917" until
  this is settled.
- **Where it trained.** The first crews trained in secret at Elveden, near Thetford. In October 1916
  the Heavy Section moved to Bovington, by Wool in Dorset, to grow to nine battalions. The camps
  around it were Wareham, Swanage and Lulworth.
- **Where it was based in France.** Headquarters was at Bermicourt, with central workshops at Erin,
  about 2.5 miles away.
- **Where the crews came from.** The first crews were volunteers, mostly from the Motor Machine Gun
  Service, the Army Service Corps (drivers) and the Navy. Individual infantrymen show up in the
  records "sent with ten others" to the Heavy Section at Wool. How many came from the infantry,
  and whether the corps called for volunteers or simply posted men, is **not yet found**.
- **How fast it grew.** It had nine battalions in 1917 and planned eighteen. Battalions were
  lettered, and so were the tank names: D17 at Flers was *Dinnaken*.

**The machine (Mark IV, 1917-18).**
- **Crew, guns, speed.** A crew of eight. The "male" carried two 6-pounders and machine guns, the
  "female" machine guns only. A 105 hp Daimler engine; about 4 mph, or less.
- **The jobs inside.** Commander, driver, two gearsmen and four gunners: attested for the Mark I,
  the same basic design, **not yet confirmed for the Mark IV**. The air inside reached 50°C, with
  carbon monoxide, petrol and oil vapour, and cordite fumes.
- **"Splash".** Bullets hitting the armour melted, and the hot metal came through the gaps between
  the plates. A splash mask was approved on 9 July 1917, and about 5,000 were issued for Cambrai:
  steel eye plates, a leather nose and a chain-mail veil. One officer remembered that it was
  laughed at, then packed with the spare kit. Surviving masks look little used.

**The battles a tank man would see.**
- **Flers-Courcelette, 15 September 1916: the first tank action.** 49 tanks were available, and
  far fewer reached the start. D17 drove into Flers, and an RFC airman's report of it became
  famous. Its exact wording differs between sources.
- **Arras and Bullecourt, April 1917.** At Bullecourt on 11 April, the tanks the Australian plan
  depended on broke down or were knocked out. The Australians blamed the tanks for years.
- **Third Ypres, from 31 July 1917.** The tanks drowned in mud. The exception was the "Cockcroft"
  action on 19 August: tanks kept to the road and took four pillboxes, with few casualties.
- **Cambrai, from 20 November 1917.** About 476 tanks; zero at 06:20, behind a barrage from
  over 1,000 guns. At the end of the first day about 180 tanks were out of action: 65 destroyed
  by the enemy, 71 broken down, 43 ditched. Flesquières ridge stopped the centre: almost 40 tanks
  were knocked out there by guns whose crews had trained against tanks.
- **Amiens, 8 August 1918.** Zero at 04:20, in fog, with over 500 tanks (SOURCES.md). The Whippet
  *Musical Box* (Lt C. B. Arnold, driver W. J. Carnie, gunner C. Ribbans) fought behind the German
  lines for about nine hours until a shell stopped it; Carnie was killed getting out, the other
  two captured.
- **The Hundred Days.** Of the 45 tanks of one force on 8 August, eight were running on 5
  November; over 120 were knocked out by artillery in ten days of August. The last Tank Corps men
  killed in action died on 4 November 1918.

**The cost to the crews.** Fuller counts 598 officers and 2,826 other ranks as casualties
against the corps' establishment, apparently for the August 1918 battles. A blog, source not
given, puts about 2,400 Tank Corps casualties between 21 August and the Armistice. **No rate per
crewman was found.** The shape the sources agree on: crews spent most of their time in camps
behind the line, and in battle the tank drew every gun on the field.

**Pay.** A Tank Corps pay warrant exists. The search extracts were garbled ("Private ... 2s. 2d."),
so **no rate is confirmed**. Treat pay as tuned.

**Not checked this session, but likely, and wanted by the story.** Church bells rang in Britain
for Cambrai, and the German counter-attack of 30 November took most of the ground back. From
November 1917, "tank banks" toured British towns selling War Bonds. In the March 1918 retreat,
crews whose tanks were lost fought on as Lewis-gun teams. Hotchkiss guns replaced Lewis guns in
tanks from 1917. Lulworth was the gunnery range. Pigeons were carried for messages.

## The design

### Who is offered it, and when

- **When.** Once, on the tour page of January 1917 (tour 20, the start of stage 3), when the Heavy
  Branch was growing to nine battalions. One offer, as with the tunneller.
- **Who.** Any town: a man with the hometown skill machinery (`mech`) or Lewis gun 2 or more; not
  already transferred, not commissioned, sergeant or below.
- **The mill most of all.** The mill picks machinery by default, so this becomes the mill's
  tunneller, answering KILLGATE open issue 3 (the mill is the weakest town and needs a strength).
  A pit or farm man with machinery, or any Lewis gunner, can be offered it too; a pit man who took
  the tunnellers is already gone.
- **The pressure.** The officer asking says what the infantry said: the tanks let them down at
  Bullecourt. The man is asked to join something the battalion jeers at.

### What he gives up

The same as the tunneller (`finishWar` and `friendLosses` already handle it): he leaves the
section for good; his three friends fight on in every battle and can die; he hears of it
secondhand; and the section's grade credit (`perFriend`) still counts who comes home.

**New: one battle a year with the battalion.** At Arras in 1917 and Amiens in 1918, his tank is
allotted to the battalion's attack. There, choosing `help` cuts his friends' loss chance that
phase by ×0.8: he can save them from inside the tank. He still does not count as `present` for
their deaths, but the scene lets him see the section through the slits.

### What changes in the war layer

**More battles, fewer tours in the line.** In 1917 the tank man fights three battles: Arras (as
Bullecourt for him), Passchendaele in the mud, and **Cambrai**, a new battle for the tank path
only: three phases (the wire, the Hindenburg Line, Flesquières) on the last tour of 1917, which
the calendar reads as November for him only. In 1918 he fights the battalion's three: the Spring
Offensive (the tanks lost, the crews fighting on with Lewis guns: the `defence` phases
unchanged), Amiens and October.

**The first two tours are training.** January and February 1917 at Wool and Bermicourt: no fire,
no missions, story screens only.

**Choices in battle.** The engine keeps `push / help / ground` and the phase types; the prose
relabels them for a tank: `push` "keep her going forward", `help` "turn her onto the gun holding
the infantry up", `ground` "pull her into dead ground and wait".

**Role missions** (`ROLES.tank`, sortie key `tank`). Each is three steps; the sortie skill is
`lewis`, and machinery gives the x1.6 aptitude. The kinds: bringing the tank up by night, taping
the route to the start line, unditching under fire, salvaging a knocked-out tank.

Proposed: chance 0.7, hit 0.05, nerve 5, award 1.2, success 0.55; shares [0.3, 0.4, 0.3], turn
back only at step 1, need 3.5. No new war skill: the count is already over the SPEC's 12.

**Pay** (`PAY.tankPence`). Rank pay plus 4d a day of corps pay. This is tuned, not sourced (the
warrant was not read), and well below the tunneller's six shillings.

**Promotion.** Merit from missions, as for every role. His guaranteed step at leave now stops at
sergeant (2026-11-14). The tank's NCO is the second in command, so a sergeant gunner reads as the
commander's right hand. A commission at the 1917 leave works as for anyone else: back in 1918 as a
second lieutenant commanding a tank.

**Awards.** As now: the MM from 1916, then the DCM and the VC.

## The story

### The crew (fiction; the tank's name to be checked against real tank names)

The tank is *Lodestar*. The crew come from elsewhere, as the tunneller's did (Evans, Pascoe, Mott):

| | Who | Thread |
|---|---|---|
| 2/Lt Edward Lacey (commander) | Twenty, a Midlands motor-works family; sick every time she moves | Learns to listen to his crew. Killed at Flesquières, or wounded and home, by the dice |
| Cpl Walter Pring (driver) | A London General omnibus driver; "drives like a bus with the brakes on" | Gets a Whippet in 1918 and asks for him as gunner |
| Pte Alf Tonks (gearsman) | Black Country foundry man; hammers on the engine cover to signal | Deaf in one ear by 1918 |
| Pte Duggie Murray (gearsman) | Glasgow riveter; says the tank is "badly riveted" | Burned at Cambrai, or not |
| Pte Mick Kearney (gunner) | Liverpool docks; the card player | Captured when a tank ditches beyond the line |

The four men are `ROLE.crew`; Lacey is the officer above them. Each has a death or wound scene,
as the pit friends do.

### 1917 (tours 20-29, plus Cambrai)

- January: the tarpaulin at Wool and the "water carrier" story; his first sight of her.
- February: Bermicourt and Erin; the gearsmen's drill; the hammer signals.
- April: Bullecourt; she breaks down short of the wire, and the Australians' faces afterwards.
- April: with the battalion at Arras; he sees the section go past the slit.
- May: a man from the battalion at the railhead says "tin can", and then asks who has the rum.
- June: the heat inside, and the first man out cold from fumes; the pigeon basket.
- August: Ypres; she sinks to the sponsons; walking back across the mud carrying the Lewis guns.
- September: the splash mask issued; Kearney wears it to deal cards.
- November: Cambrai. The fascine dropped into the Hindenburg trench, the wire flattened, then the
  guns on Flesquières ridge.
- Both roads: a letter from home asking whether he is "in one of those".

### 1918 (tours 30-39)

- January: the Mark V promised; a camp concert; tanks being painted for the spring.
- February: Pring asks for him on a Whippet (a choice: the Whippet's crew of three, or *Lodestar*).
- March: the tanks lost in the retreat, burned so the enemy cannot have them; the crew as a
  Lewis-gun team.
- April: a draft of infantrymen from a disbanded battalion joins the corps (to check).
- July: the move up to Amiens at night, the engines muffled.
- August: Amiens in fog with the battalion behind him, and a Whippet loose in the German rear.
  This is the *Musical Box* shape, fictional crew.
- September: Tonks's ear; Lacey's replacement is younger than the crew.
- October: few tanks left; booby traps in the Hindenburg dugouts.
- Both roads: the July letter from home.

### Leaves and the end

- **The 1917 leave.** The town has heard the bells for Cambrai, and then the news that the ground
  was lost. Everyone wants to know what a tank is like inside. The "tank bank" is coming to the
  town hall.
- **The 1918 Armistice.** It finds him at a tankodrome with nothing left to drive. A short coda
  per town: the mill's buzzer, the pit's buzzer, the farm's bells, the City's paper from the
  windows.
- **Word from the battalion.** A battalion man at the start line or the railhead; their letters
  home, read back to him in his mother's; once a year, his own eyes from inside the tank. The
  tour-page lines "Word comes over from the battalion..." gain a tank take ("at the tankodrome").

## Measurement

**The gate's targets** (SPEC, 2026-11-08) must still hold: best play reaches the Armistice in
5-10% of runs, reckless near 0%, competent play survives 1915 in 60-75%. The offer comes after
1915, so the 1915 row cannot move: if it does, something leaked.

**A new gate table, "The tank transfer"** (eligible men, 1,000 runs, ± 1 SE), in the same form as
the tunneller's row in KILLGATE.md. Columns: takes it vs refuses it, for Armistice, killed,
captured, home alive, friends home, and grade.
- **The pass:** a real trade, not best on both survival and grade. **The shape wanted:** about
  the infantry's killed rate, more awards and grade, fewer friends home, more captures.
- **The mill's row** in the town table should rise, without becoming best on both.

**Story share.** `npx tsx tools/story-share.ts 200 mill`, on each road, near half story in 1917
and in 1918 (target 45-60%). The tunneller's 1918 was 70%: missions on 70% of tours are there to
stop that.

**The usual checks.** Transcripts of a full tank road read end to end; the validator, style lint,
continuity (the crew guarded like friends) and the render scan.

## Open questions for the author

1. **Who is offered it?** Recommended: any town, with machinery or Lewis gun 2 or more, so the mill
   gets its own road. The alternative is the mill only, which mirrors the pit-only tunneller.
2. **Is Cambrai a battle for the tank man?** Recommended: yes, a third 1917 battle for him only,
   balanced by fewer tours in the line. It is the tank's battle, and the infantry layer has no
   November. The alternative is an event only, as Messines is for the tunneller.
3. **A second offer in early 1918?** Recommended: no. One offer, as with the tunneller; a choice
   that comes back loses its weight.
4. **The Whippet in 1918?** Recommended: a story choice with flags and `war_mood` only, and no new
   numbers. The Whippet's own risk is not worth a second tank path.
